# Recruiter Workflow and Modes

## Modes

- **Query planning:** formulate source-specific, role-relevant search terms. Label all queries as proposed until actually run using an available permitted tool.
- **Discovery:** identify possible public professional artifacts. Treat findings as leads, not verified facts or a shortlist.
- **Verification:** check a specific attributed claim and identity linkage; report source evidence and uncertainty.
- **Dossier organization:** normalize evidence in the [dossier template](talent-dossier-template.md), preserve unknowns, and avoid evaluative conclusions.
- **Structured follow-up preparation:** draft neutral questions or work-sample topics to validate job-related claims.
- **Professional outreach:** offer optional low-pressure wording for an appropriate professional channel; stop after any decline or request not to be contacted.

## Routing menu

Choose a mode to change research scope and output only:

1. **Quick discovery:** narrow query plan and a small set of source types.
2. **Deep research:** broader evidence review with explicit provenance, gaps, and conflicts.
3. **Passive-talent discovery:** identify role-relevant professional artifacts; never infer job-seeking status or availability.
4. **Hidden-talent/source expansion:** broaden permitted public professional source categories; do not imply exhaustive coverage.
5. **Professional-branding review:** summarize stated professional positioning and artifacts, not popularity or personality.
6. **Public professional-content review:** review only accessible, permitted, role-relevant public work content.
7. **Talent mapping:** organize a bounded, non-exhaustive map by declared scope and evidence.
8. **Executive research:** summarize public professional roles, board/company materials, talks, or publications; no private-life investigation.
9. **Technical discovery:** plan around public repositories, project artifacts, talks, or technical publications.
10. **Public-source competitor landscape mapping:** map organizations, public role descriptions, and professional capability themes—not private employees or individuals ranked against each other.
11. **Purpose-limited talent-pool schema:** organize minimized records and governance fields using [research-module data-model guidance](research-modules.md).
12. **Talent-market intelligence:** describe source coverage and observed themes within a stated scope; no invented market counts or claims of completeness.

Modes do not enable platform access, automation permissions, mass collection, candidate ranking, or hiring decisions. Apply the same source-access and safety limits in every mode.

## Sequence

1. **Scope:** document role, essential job-related criteria, geography only if relevant, source categories, access limitations, and exclusions.
2. **Plan:** create narrow illustrative queries; do not claim results without an observed search.
3. **Discover:** record source artifacts, not personal profiles for their own sake.
4. **Verify:** handle identity ambiguity, recency, provenance, and conflicts explicitly.
5. **Organize:** create neutral, attributed entries. Separate verified facts, self-claims, and unknowns.
6. **Human assessment:** leave employment assessment to authorized humans using consistent, structured, job-related criteria. No ranking, scoring, shortlist, recommendation, or automated rejection.
7. **Contact:** only if appropriate, by a professional channel, with an easy opt-out. Stop on decline; do not follow up through personal channels.

## Neutral validation prompts

- “Could you walk us through your role and contribution to this public project?”
- “What constraints and trade-offs shaped the work shown in this case study?”
- “Would you be willing to complete the same role-related work sample used for other candidates?”

These prompts seek clarification; they do not presume a claim is true or predict performance.
