# Research Modules

These modules extend the core workflow. Keep outputs descriptive, source-attributed, and suitable for human review. They do not duplicate the dossier schema, evidence-status definitions, or privacy rules in the linked [dossier template](talent-dossier-template.md), [evidence verification](evidence-verification.md), and [ethical OSINT guidance](ethical-osint-privacy.md).

## Professional branding and public professional content

Describe observable professional positioning and artifacts: topics addressed, knowledge shared, project contributions, and how the person describes their professional focus. Attribute each observation to a public professional source; distinguish stated positioning from independently supported evidence. Exclude personality, character, virality, popularity, follower counts, likes, and engagement metrics. Social content is in scope only when relevant to work and accessible under the source’s rules and the stated purpose.

## Skills verification

Translate each requested skill into a specific claim and suitable evidence type (for example, a project artifact, publication, public contribution, credential, or structured work sample). Distinguish self-description from independently supported evidence. A public artifact may not establish individual authorship, depth, recency, or production use; record those as limitations and use consistent human-led follow-up.

## Career trajectory

Summarize only explicitly documented professional roles, projects, learning, or transitions with source attribution and dates. Do not infer motivation, personal circumstances, future trajectory, readiness, or likely performance. Treat possible strengths or gaps as hypotheses for structured work samples/interviews, not predictions.

## Evidence-based matching (descriptive only)

Map role criteria to observed evidence in a neutral matrix: criterion; source-backed evidence; evidence status; date/recency; limitations; unknowns. Use labels such as “evidence observed,” “not observed in reviewed sources,” and “unclear”; absence is not negative evidence. Do not calculate fit scores, rank people, create a shortlist, or recommend hire/reject. Human reviewers retain all assessment decisions and should use consistent job-related criteria.

## Talent mapping and talent-pool data model

For a defined, bounded talent-market map, state geography, role/topic, source categories, time window, and inclusion scope. Mark the map as non-exhaustive, describe access/tool coverage, and never invent population or search-result counts. Keep separate, minimized records for:

- **Candidate record:** stable internal ID, public professional identity label, identity ambiguity, role-related scope, and record purpose.
- **Professional profile:** attributed roles, projects, skills, career dates, professional positioning, topics, and public knowledge-sharing artifacts.
- **Branding content:** artifact/source link, title, topic, date, stated positioning, and contribution context; no popularity or personality fields.
- **Evidence:** claim, source title/URL, access and publication dates, excerpt/paraphrase, verification status, confidence reason, recency, conflicts, unknowns, and limitations.
- **Descriptive matching:** role criterion, linked evidence, status, and unresolved questions; never scores, rank, or shortlist people.
- **Outreach:** professional channel type, approved message/context, date/status, opt-out/decline, and suppression from further contact; never store private contact details.
- **Privacy/governance:** purpose, source/method permissions, data-minimization note, authorized reviewer access, retention/deletion date, and applicable policy review.

Keep source artifacts distinct from derived summaries and avoid duplicate identities or unnecessary personal data. A talent pool is a purpose-limited evidence index, not a ranked candidate database. Apply the [privacy guidance](ethical-osint-privacy.md) and [evidence verification](evidence-verification.md).

## Reporting template

Include purpose and role criteria; mapping scope, time window, and non-exhaustive coverage; sources/methods actually used (or “query plan only”); tool/access limits; evidence summary with citations; profile/branding claims and verification state; descriptive criterion-to-evidence matrix; ambiguity, conflicts, unknowns, and limitations; outreach/opt-out status where relevant; privacy, access, and retention handling; and neutral next-step questions. Report only observed counts with a clear method and coverage note; do not imply completeness or invent counts. Never end with a hiring recommendation.

## Potential hypotheses

If professional evidence suggests a possible strength or development area, label it as a hypothesis tied to named evidence and uncertainty. Do not predict performance or trajectory. Suggest validating it with a consistent structured work sample or interview question.

## Tool and error handling

Before using a search or research tool, confirm it is actually available and permitted for the requested source. If unavailable, say so and provide a manual query plan. If access is denied, source is incomplete, or an error occurs, stop that source attempt; report the observed limitation without bypassing controls, switching to unauthorized scraping, or inventing substitute results. Preserve only necessary error details, distinguish tool failure from lack of evidence, and keep search planning separate from executed searches.
