# Evidence Verification

## Record what was observed

For each material professional claim, preserve the source title and URL, access date (if actually accessed), excerpt or faithful paraphrase, evidence type, recency, identity ambiguity, conflicts, and limitations. Distinguish an individual’s self-description from a primary artifact and from independent corroboration.

## Verification states

- **Unverified:** assertion is present, but not independently checked.
- **Partially verified:** some details match an identified source; scope remains limited.
- **Corroborated:** independent, relevant evidence supports the specific claim; identify both sources and what each establishes.
- **Conflicted:** sources disagree; show both accounts and do not resolve by guessing.
- **Unresolved:** identity, access, recency, or evidence quality prevents a reliable conclusion.

Confidence describes evidence strength for the stated claim only; it is not candidate quality or job fit. State the reason (source provenance, independence, specificity, recency, identity linkage). Avoid numerical scores.

## Review sequence

1. Verify source provenance and whether the content was actually accessible through an allowed method.
2. Check whether the source identifies the same professional person; names, avatars, and handles alone may be ambiguous.
3. Verify the exact claim, not a broader interpretation. A repository contribution does not by itself establish authorship, employment, seniority, or impact.
4. Compare independent sources where available; detect copied bios, stale pages, and conflicting dates/roles.
5. State recency and limitations. Missing evidence is unknown, not negative evidence.
6. Route unresolved claims to human verification through a fair, structured work sample or interview, not an employment recommendation.
